<Update label="October 26, 2026">

## New features

**Stream: `update` event for price changes and late float / pattern**

[`GET /v1/listings/stream`](/api-reference/get-listings-stream) now sends `update` when a listing you already received changes price, or gets `float_value` / `paint_seed` filled in (some markets send these a few minutes after the lot goes on sale). The payload is the full `Listing`, same as `new`: replace your copy by `id`. With the [snapshot](/api-reference/get-listings-snapshot) plus `new` / `update` / `gone` you can keep the whole catalogue on your side instead of polling `GET /v1/listings`. `min_price` / `max_price` do not filter `update`, so a listing you hold that reprices out of your range still reaches you. Clients that ignore unknown event types keep working unchanged.

**Why an order failed: `fail_reason`, `fail_side`, `fail_message`**

Orders now say why they failed, in [`GET /v1/orders`](/api-reference/get-orders), [`GET /v1/orders/info`](/api-reference/get-orders-info), [`GET /v1/orders/{id}`](/api-reference/get-order) and the `order.updated` webhook, P2P rows included. On a `failed` or `cancelled` order, `fail_reason` is a code to branch on, `fail_side` says who has to act (`recipient`, `seller`, `buyer` or `market`), and `fail_message` is one sentence saying what happened and what to do. All three are `null` on other statuses. Until now such an order read only as `unavailable`, even when the recipient's inventory was private — a case only you can fix. The list of codes may grow: treat an unknown value as `unavailable`. See [why an order failed](/api-reference/get-orders#fail-reasons).

**Rate limit headers on every answer**

Every answer to a keyed call carries `X-RateLimit-Limit`, `X-RateLimit-Remaining`, `X-RateLimit-Reset` and `X-RateLimit-Bucket` for the bucket it spent, so you can pace yourself before a `429`. [`GET /v1/key`](/api-reference/get-key) lists every limit of the key under `rate_limits`.

## Changes

- Keyed calls are now metered only by the key's own buckets. A site-wide per-IP limit of about 133 requests/min used to count them as well, so it capped every integration below the per-key numbers documented here, and its `x-ratelimit-limit: 1000` header did not describe your key. Keyless endpoints (`GET /v1/health`, `GET /v1/public/prices`) are still limited per IP. See [Rate limits](/rate-limits).
- The trading bucket is 3,000 requests/min per key, or the key's read limit if that is higher. The 10,000/min announced on October 20 could not be reached because of the per-IP limit above; 3,000 is the ceiling actually in force.
- A `429 rate_limit_exceeded` from a per-key bucket names it: `bucket` (`read`, `trading` or `buy_orders`), `limit_per_min` and `retry_after_sec`. `Retry-After` is now the seconds left until the window restarts (1–60) instead of a flat 60 — here and on `snapshot_rate_limited`, the P2P write limit and the Instant Sell quote limit.
- `429 too_many_streams` carries `Retry-After: 90`, `max_connections` and `retry_after_sec`: a stream that dropped without closing frees its slot within 90 seconds.
- `409 item_unavailable` on CS2 buys names the ids we have no record of in `unknown_ids`: not a listing id (a Steam asset id is the usual mix-up), or a listing gone from the catalogue for over an hour. `unavailable_ids` still lists every missing id.

</Update>

<Update label="October 22, 2026">

## New features

**Manual delivery is flagged on every order**

Orders now carry `delivery_type` (`instant` | `manual`) and `delivery_deadline_at` in [`GET /v1/orders`](/api-reference/get-orders), [`GET /v1/orders/info`](/api-reference/get-orders-info), [`GET /v1/orders/{id}`](/api-reference/get-order) and the `order.updated` webhook, P2P rows included. A `manual` order is sent by a person and can take up to 12 hours, so it may sit in `delivering` with no trade offer that long. `delivery_deadline_at` is set while the order is `pending` or `delivering`; if the seller misses it, the order is cancelled and refunded automatically.

**Cancel an undelivered order: `POST /v1/orders/{id}/cancel`**

[`POST /v1/orders/{id}/cancel`](/api-reference/post-orders-cancel) calls off an order nobody has shipped yet and refunds it to your balance. It opens 31 minutes after purchase for manual delivery and 15 minutes for everything else; earlier calls answer `425 too_early` with `retry_after_sec`. Player (P2P) lots cannot be cancelled here.

</Update>

<Update label="October 20, 2026">

## New features

**One item's whole book, live: `GET /v1/listings/depth`**

[`GET /v1/listings/depth`](/api-reference/get-listings-depth) takes a `market_hash_name`, refreshes that item's book from the upstream market first (your `min_float` / `max_float`, `phase` and `delivery` are passed through), then returns every lot we hold for the name, cheapest first, in the same `Listing` shape as `/v1/listings`. Every `id` goes straight into [`POST /v1/orders`](/api-reference/post-orders).

- `refresh` on the response says whether the live refresh happened (`ok`) and, if not, why (`budget`, `timeout`, `unavailable`, `delivery`). Without it you still get the book as of its last sighting.
- The shelf itself is now far deeper: per-asset stock from our Chinese supply is kept at up to 1,000 lots per item (was 30) and floats are filled in continuously, so `/v1/listings?name=`, the snapshot and the stream carry the whole book, not the cheapest few.
- `/v1/listings` gained a `doppler_phase` filter (exact phase, as the response carries it).

**The whole shelf in one file: `GET /v1/listings/snapshot.ndjson.gz`**

[`GET /v1/listings/snapshot.ndjson.gz`](/api-reference/get-listings-snapshot) is every buyable listing as gzipped NDJSON, one `Listing` per line in the exact shape `/v1/listings` returns. Download it once, then open [`/v1/listings/stream`](/api-reference/get-listings-stream) with `last_event_id` set to the file's `X-Stream-Position` header and your copy stays current from there. This replaces walking the catalogue page by page.

- Rebuilt every 10 minutes while anyone is downloading it. `ETag` / `Last-Modified` with `If-None-Match` / `If-Modified-Since` answer `304` when nothing changed.
- Same one-listing-per-physical-skin rule as `/v1/listings`, same negotiated discount applied.
- Six full downloads per hour per key; a `304` is free.

## Changes

- Trading calls (`POST /v1/orders`, `POST /v1/market/buy`, `GET /v1/orders*`, `POST /v1/orders/{id}/claim`, `GET /v1/balance`) now count against their own per-key budget of 10,000 requests/min instead of the 100/min read budget, so paging the catalogue can no longer block a buy or an order-status poll. See [Rate limits](/rate-limits).
- The stream keeps about two hours of history (was ~15 minutes at current volume) and a reconnect replays the whole gap, however long, instead of the first 5,000 events. The `resync` event now names the snapshot as the way back in.

## Fixes

- `price_usd` on `/v1/listings`, `/v1/listings/depth`, the stream and the snapshot is now exactly what the buy charges. Lots from about $100 whose price we lift toward the item's next-cheapest listing were shown at the pre-lift price, so sending that number as `max_price_usd` was refused with `price_moved` every time. If you saw `price_moved` repeat on the same item, this was it.

</Update>

<Update label="October 14, 2026">

## New features

**Buy Dota 2 and Rust with delivery to any trade URL**

[`POST /v1/market/buy`](/api-reference/post-market-buy) now takes `appId` in the body, like [`POST /v1/orders`](/api-reference/post-orders): `570` for Dota 2, `252490` for Rust. Leave it out and the endpoint buys CS2, as before.

- Payment and delivery work as they do for CS2: settled balance only, and the skins go to the trade URL built from `partner` + `token`.
- A Dota 2 or Rust order takes up to 10 ids. More answers `400 too_many_items`.
- Dota 2 and Rust orders are queued, so every skin answers `pending` first. Follow the order with [`GET /v1/orders/info`](/api-reference/get-orders-info) or the `order.updated` webhook.

</Update>

<Update label="October 6, 2026">

## New features

**Instant Sell for Dota 2 and Rust, and sales across several offers**

- [`POST /v1/sell/quotes`](/api-reference/post-sell-quotes) and [`POST /v1/sell/orders`](/api-reference/post-sell-orders) take `app_id`: `730` CS2 (the default), `570` Dota 2, `252490` Rust. A game your key cannot sell answers `422 unsupported_game` with `supported_app_ids`. Quote items and orders carry `app_id`.
- New `split` flag. Leave it `false` and the sale ships as one trade offer, to the market that leaves the seller the most value. Set it `true` and every item goes to the market that pays the most for it, which can mean several offers. Quote items carry `offer_group` (`a`, `b`, and so on) so you know how many offers to expect.
- Orders now carry `trade_offer_ids`, an `offer_group` on each offer and a `status` on each item. The `sell.updated` webhook sends the same object.
- Credit timing per game: CS2 about 7 days after the seller accepts, Dota 2 7 days after the seller accepts, Rust as soon as the market confirms. See [when the money arrives](/guides/instant-sell#when-the-money-arrives).
- `min_total_usd` is now checked against the total the order actually books, after the items are grouped into offers. Below it, the order is refused with `409 price_drift`.
- The Instant Sell endpoints have their own pages in the API reference, including [`GET /v1/sell/orders`](/api-reference/get-sell-orders) with list mode and pagination.

**Webhooks for sales, withdrawals and deposits**

The [webhooks guide](/guides/webhooks#event-types) now documents `sell.updated`, `withdrawal.updated` and `deposit.updated` with their payloads.

</Update>

<Update label="October 2, 2026">

## New features

**Dota 2 and Rust over the same API — choose the game with `appId`**

CSBoard now sells Dota 2 and Rust items as well as CS2, and the API reaches them through one new parameter. Pass Steam's appId — `570` for Dota 2, `252490` for Rust — and the endpoint answers for that game. Leave it out and you get CS2, byte for byte as before.

- `appId` works on [`GET /v1/listings`](/api-reference/get-listings), [`GET /v1/prices`](/api-reference/get-prices) and [`GET /v1/listings/availability`](/api-reference/get-listings-availability) as a query parameter, and on [`POST /v1/orders`](/api-reference/post-orders) as a body field.
- Dota 2 and Rust rows have the same keys as CS2 rows. The CS2-only fields are `null` and `stickers` is `[]`, so one parser reads all three games.
- CS2-only filters (`wear`, `min_float`, `max_float`, `stat_trak`, `souvenir`, `available_after`, `sort=newest`) sent with another game answer `400 invalid_param` instead of being silently ignored.
- An `appId` your key cannot use answers `400 unsupported_game` with `supported_app_ids`. New endpoint [**`GET /v1/games`**](/api-reference/get-games) lists the games your key can read and whether it can buy there.
- Dota 2 and Rust orders answer `delivery: "pending"` and `expected_minutes: null`.

The SSE stream, the bulk snapshot and `POST /v1/market/buy` stay CS2 only.

**A keyless price feed: `GET /v1/public/prices`**

[**`GET /v1/public/prices`**](/api-reference/get-public-prices) returns one cheapest ask per `market_hash_name` for CS2, Dota 2 or Rust. It needs no API key and no balance. CS2 Doppler phases are merged into Steam's base name, so every key matches a real Steam name. The feed is rebuilt once a minute and limited to 1 request per minute per IP.

**List your own skins on the P2P market from the API — free**

Five new endpoints let a key put its owner's CS2 items up for sale on the CSBoard P2P market. No balance requirement and no trading permission — any valid key works.

- [`GET /v1/p2p/inventory`](/api-reference/get-p2p-inventory) — your items, which can be listed now, and why the others cannot.
- [`POST /v1/p2p/listings`](/api-reference/post-p2p-listings) — publish one item or up to 50; `Idempotency-Key` required.
- [`GET /v1/p2p/listings`](/api-reference/get-p2p-listings), [`PATCH /v1/p2p/listings/{id}`](/api-reference/patch-p2p-listing) and [`DELETE /v1/p2p/listings/{id}`](/api-reference/delete-p2p-listing) — see, reprice and remove them.

The website's seller rules apply unchanged, and a sold item still has to be sent through Steam and confirmed with Steam Guard before the payout reaches your balance. One deliberate difference: a price under half of our market price is refused with `422 price_below_market`. Over a key it cannot be confirmed past, so a leaked key cannot dump your inventory. Writes are limited to 30 per minute per key. The [P2P listing guide](/guides/p2p-listing) walks through the whole flow.

</Update>

<Update label="August 5, 2026">

## Fixes

**Purchases that time out now answer `504`, not `500`.** When a marketplace stops responding mid-purchase the outcome is genuinely unknown — the buy may still have completed. `POST /market/buy` and `POST /orders` now return `504 upstream_timeout` for that case and say so explicitly.

Crucially, the idempotency claim is **held** instead of released. Re-sending the identical `custom_id` / `Idempotency-Key` replays the original outcome rather than buying a second time; a retry sent while the original is still unresolved returns `409 idempotency_in_progress`. Poll the order list before treating a `504` as a failure. Deterministic errors (price moved, insufficient balance, item unavailable) are unchanged.


**Lost concurrency races answer `409 concurrent_update`.** When two orders touch the same items at the same instant the loser's transaction is rolled back whole — nothing charged, nothing reserved. That used to surface as `500`; it is now a `409` with `Retry-After`, because retrying is exactly the right move.
</Update>

<Update label="July 27, 2026">

## New features

**Stop polling for new listings — subscribe to them**

Until today the only way to notice new inventory was to poll [`GET /v1/listings`](/api-reference/get-listings) harder, and that cost does not divide across clients: ten integrators at 300 requests/minute is ten separate deep scans of the same table returning almost identical rows. Worse, the thing everyone was polling could not answer the question — `sort=newest` ordered by when the *row* was created, so items returning to the catalogue from cancelled orders, expired trade offers and released holds never surfaced at the top, no matter how fast anyone polled.

Three additions, all backward compatible:

- New endpoint [**`GET /v1/listings/stream`**](/api-reference/get-listings-stream) — Server-Sent Events. One connection replaces any polling loop, and it emits **`gone`** events as well as `new` ones. That second half is what polling cannot express: a poller only discovers an item sold by trying to buy it and failing. Reconnects are gap-free via the standard `Last-Event-ID` header, including across our deploys.
- New query parameter **`available_after`** on `GET /v1/listings` — turns a poll into a delta read. The usual response is an empty page. Overlap your watermark by ~60 seconds and dedupe by `id`; the parameter docs explain why that overlap is not optional.
- New field **`listed_at`** on every `Listing` — when the item entered the catalogue, which is what `sort=newest` now orders by. Re-listings surface alongside genuinely new inventory.

Nothing existing changes shape: `sort=newest` keeps its name and its ordering direction, it simply stopped hiding re-listings.

## Fixes

**Corrected rate limits in the docs**

The published limits were wrong in the direction that costs you throughput: pointed reads are **100 requests/minute**, not 30, and `POST /v1/orders` is **250/minute**, not 30. If you sized your client against the old numbers, you have more headroom than you thought — and if you were about to ask for a raise, try `available_after` or the stream first.

</Update>

<Update label="July 14, 2026">

## New features

**Refunds are now explicit — no more guessing**

An order that ends `cancelled` or `failed` has always been refunded in full, usually within seconds. The API just never said so, which left clients rendering "refund pending" against orders that were already settled. Two additions fix that:

- Every order — in [`GET /v1/orders`](/api-reference/get-orders), [`GET /v1/orders/{id}`](/api-reference/get-order) and [`GET /v1/orders/info`](/api-reference/get-orders-info) — now carries a **`refund`** object: `amount_usd`, `refunded_at`, and `partial` (true when only one leg of a multi-item order came back). It is `null` when nothing was refunded.
- New endpoint [**`GET /v1/transactions`**](/api-reference/get-transactions) — your full balance ledger, keyset paginated: `purchase`, `refund`, `sale`, `deposit`, `withdrawal`, `bonus`, `fee`, `adjustment`. A purchase and the refund that reversed it share the same `order_id`, so an end-of-day reconciliation is one pass over this feed.

Both are additive — the `status` vocabulary is unchanged, so existing integrations keep working untouched.

</Update>

<Update label="Week of July 6, 2026">

## New features

**Instant Sell API (invite-only)**

Sell CS2 skins programmatically through the CSBoard bot network. Quote a Steam inventory, create a sell order, and funds settle to your CSBoard balance once Steam's trade-protection hold clears (~8 days).

- Quote any Steam inventory with [`POST /v1/sell/quotes`](/guides/instant-sell) — every eligible item comes back with the exact USD amount you'll be credited.
- Create orders with [`POST /v1/sell/orders`](/guides/instant-sell), with optional `min_total_usd` price-drift protection and `external_id` idempotency.
- Track lifecycle (`pending` → `offer_sent` → `received` → `completed`) and settled amounts with [`GET /v1/sell/orders/:id`](/guides/instant-sell). Large baskets can split across up to 3 bots.
- Every offer exposes the sending bot's Steam profile and `trade_offer_id` so you can render anti-impersonation checks to sellers.
- Access is granted per-account — call [`GET /v1/sell/status`](/guides/instant-sell) to check, and contact support to request access.

See the [Instant Sell guide](/guides/instant-sell) for the full walkthrough.

</Update>

<Update label="July 5, 2026">

## Fixes

**Precise 402 error codes on `POST /v1/market/buy`**

The endpoint now distinguishes the two out-of-funds cases instead of always returning `insufficient_settled_balance`:

- `insufficient_balance` — the account balance is simply lower than the order total. Top up and retry.
- `insufficient_settled_balance` — the balance covers the total, but part of it is still inside the reversal window and cannot fund external delivery yet.

Both responses now include `balance_usd` alongside `required_usd` and `settled_usd`, so a bot can tell the cases apart programmatically. If your integration matched on the `insufficient_settled_balance` code for generic low-funds handling, match on HTTP 402 instead.

Held orders also gained a small post-unlock grace before `claimable` flips to `true` — claiming at the exact unlock instant previously failed on the marketplace side.

</Update>

<Update label="Week of June 29, 2026">

## New features

**Claim held orders over the API**

Orders that land under a marketplace hold (`status: "hold"` with a `hold_until` timestamp) can now be released programmatically. Once `hold_until` passes, call [`POST /v1/orders/:id/claim`](/api-reference/post-orders-claim) to release the order. After a successful claim the marketplace sends a Steam trade offer your bot must accept within ~15 minutes. Some held orders deliver automatically, in which case the call is a harmless no-op.

**`autoclaim` account setting**

Set `autoclaim: true` on [`POST /v1/orders`](/api-reference/post-orders) or [`POST /v1/market/buy`](/api-reference/post-market-buy) and every held order is released automatically the moment its hold clears — no per-order call required. The flag is sticky at the account level.

**Exact `name` filter on listings and prices**

Pass a `name` query parameter to [`GET /v1/listings`](/api-reference/get-listings) or [`GET /v1/prices`](/api-reference/get-prices) to fetch a single item by its exact `market_hash_name` (case-insensitive). Unlike `search`, which does fuzzy word matching and can collide on overlapping titles (for example, `Spectrum Case` vs `Spectrum 2 Case`), `name` returns only the item you asked for. When both parameters are supplied, `name` takes precedence — no more post-filtering the response.

See [Market Data](/guides/market-data) for usage examples.

</Update>
